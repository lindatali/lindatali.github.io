The Case

Closing the Loop at Quince

Quince grew fast on viral products like its cashmere sweater, but most first-time buyers never come back. The brief: rework a $120M marketing budget to lift repeat purchase and lower blended CAC, with no discounting, and back every claim with evidence from Adobe Customer Journey Analytics.

$14M vs $6M

Renting, Not Owning

Quince spent 2.3x more on paid retargeting than on email, SMS and app combined.

43%

Unreachable Customers

Almost half of customers bought on web without subscribing, so Quince had no free way to bring them back.

7.4%

The Wedge That Doesn't Repeat

Cashmere brought in 38% of first buyers but had the lowest repeat rate of any category.

Our Recommendation

Own the Second Order

"Quince doesn't have a loyalty problem. It has a second-order problem."
Its best acquisition product brings in customers it can't reach and never shows them a second category.
01

Fund

Stop paying to retarget people Quince can already message for free, and shift part of prospecting spend into owned channels. Pure reallocation inside the existing budget.

No new spend
02

Capture

Get every first buyer into email, SMS or the app at checkout and through a QR code on existing packaging. Reachability was the strongest predictor of repeat in the data.

3x to 4x repeat
03

Bridge

Move cashmere buyers to Home within 90 days using material-led recommendations: cashmere sweater to cashmere throw, silk blouse to silk pillowcase. A story only Quince can tell.

Cashmere to Home

What the Data Showed

Findings from Adobe CJA

The competition's simulated Quince dataset in Adobe CJA held 60,000 people, 2,592 customers and 2,863 orders. Groups were small, so I used CJA for ratios and comparisons and only trusted gaps big enough to survive the noise.

Owned-channel customers are 57% of customers but 82% of repeat buyers.

Nobody shows cashmere buyers anything else

Only 44 of 983 cashmere buyers ever viewed another category, and just 15 viewed Home. The bridge has to create interest, not harvest it.

Last-touch reporting punishes the right move

Under algorithmic attribution, email gained 58% more credit and SMS 124%, while retargeting lost 6%. Owned channels go from looking 11% more expensive per order to 27% cheaper.

The gap holds over time

January app cohorts repeated at 21.8% vs 7.9% for web-only customers, and app users started coming back in the first month.

The funnel leak isn't a device problem

77% of carts were abandoned, and mobile web and desktop converted identically. The fix is follow-up, not a site redesign.
